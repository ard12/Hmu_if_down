# Title: A Regulatory-Compliant Edge AI System for Privacy-Preserving Fall Detection: Bridging RF Digital Twins, Multi-Occupant Kinematics, and Explainability

## Abstract

Ambient assisted living (AAL) increasingly relies on privacy-preserving Radio Frequency (RF) sensing—such as Wi-Fi Channel State Information (CSI) and mmWave radar—to continuously monitor vulnerable populations without the ethical compromises of optical cameras. However, transitioning empirical RF machine learning models into clinical practice is obstructed by three critical translational bottlenecks: (1) severe model degradation due to topological domain shift in novel multipath environments, (2) catastrophic kinematic interference in multi-occupant scenarios (e.g., caregiver interventions), and (3) a profound lack of regulatory-compliant explainability required for Medical Device Software (SaMD). This paper presents a holistic, edge-accelerated RF fall detection architecture that systematically resolves these barriers. To mitigate domain shift, we introduce a physics-informed 3D Digital Twin utilizing the Image-Source Method (ISM) to synthesize posture-dependent Rayleigh fading signatures, enabling zero-shot environmental adaptation. To resolve multi-target occlusion, we propose a spatiotemporal disambiguation engine combining Hungarian bipartite matching with a 6-state Kalman filter ($x, y, z, v_x, v_y, v_z$), successfully isolating anomalous vertical descent ($z < 0.5$m, $v_z < -1.5$m/s) strictly to the falling occupant. Furthermore, we embed a Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) feature attribution pipeline and gradient-based counterfactual generator natively optimized for INT8 TinyML edge execution. Deep verification of the system demonstrates strict mathematical convergence of the SHAP efficiency axiom (max residual error $< 2 \times 10^{-5}$) and 100% adherence to IEC 62304 and ISO 14971 traceability matrices across 30 clinical hazards. By bridging physics-driven simulation with explainable edge AI, this work charts a scalable blueprint for the clinical certification of RF-based continuous monitoring systems.

## 1. Introduction

The rapid expansion of the global aging population has precipitated an urgent clinical mandate for automated, continuous fall detection systems. Falls remain the leading etiology of fatal and non-fatal injuries among geriatric demographics, often resulting in prolonged hospitalizations, loss of independence, and high mortality rates if intervention is delayed. Traditional telemetry solutions rely predominantly on wearable inertial measurement units (IMUs) or optical camera networks. However, wearables suffer from notoriously low longitudinal compliance rates—particularly among populations with cognitive decline—while optical sensors severely violate patient privacy, precluding their deployment in high-risk zones such as bathrooms and private wards.

Over the past decade, Radio Frequency (RF) sensing has emerged as a disruptive, privacy-preserving paradigm. By analyzing the micro-Doppler shifts and multipath signal distortions (e.g., Wi-Fi Channel State Information) induced by human movement, deep neural networks can extract highly granular biomechanical kinematics without capturing optical images. Despite achieving near-perfect accuracy in controlled laboratory settings, the translation of theoretical RF models into trusted, regulatory-approved Medical Device Software (SaMD) remains severely bottlenecked by three systemic vulnerabilities:

**1. Topological Domain Shift:** 
Empirical RF models are highly overfit to the multipath fading profile of their training environment. When deployed in a novel clinical room, differing geometries, wall dielectric properties, and furniture configurations alter the RF propagation paths, leading to catastrophic inference failure. Collecting exhaustive empirical fall datasets across every potential deployment topology is both economically and ethically intractable.

**2. Multi-Occupant Kinematic Interference:** 
The vast majority of existing RF fall detection frameworks operate under a strict single-occupant assumption. In real-world environments, the presence of a second individual—such as a cohabitant, a pet, or a caregiver rushing to assist—generates overlapping Doppler signatures. This dynamic clutter confuses classical classifiers, resulting in a high incidence of false positives and subsequent "alarm fatigue" among nursing personnel.

**3. The Explainability Gap in Clinical Regulation:** 
Deep learning architectures are inherently opaque. Regulatory bodies, guided by frameworks such as the FDA's Good Machine Learning Practice (GMLP) and IEC 62304, demand rigorous algorithmic transparency, traceability, and risk mitigation. A "black box" model that outputs a binary binary fall classification cannot be safely integrated into clinical workflows if it cannot deterministically justify the specific spatiotemporal features driving its prediction.

### 1.1 Contributions

In this work, we propose a comprehensive, edge-accelerated Fall Detection System that addresses these translational bottlenecks through a synthesis of physics-informed RF simulation, multi-target signal processing, and Explainable AI (XAI). Our specific contributions are:

1. **Physics-Informed RF Digital Twins for Zero-Shot Adaptation:** We develop a 3D ray-tracing simulation engine based on the Image-Source Method (ISM). By modeling frequency-dependent boundary reflections and posture-specific human ellipsoidal attenuation, the system generates synthetic, room-specific CSI signatures, allowing the neural network to adapt to novel geometries prior to physical deployment.
2. **Robust Multi-Occupant Spatiotemporal Disambiguation:** We implement a real-time target association pipeline utilizing Hungarian linear assignment over a 6-state Kalman filter. This architecture dynamically isolates overlapping trajectories, successfully differentiating the sharp negative vertical velocity of a falling patient from the ambient movement of a caregiver.
3. **Translational Edge-AI Explainability:** We introduce an edge-optimized SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) attribution engine and counterfactual generator. We mathematically verify its adherence to local accuracy axioms (reconstruction error $< 2 \times 10^{-5}$), providing clinicians with deterministic, FDA-compliant feature importance vectors for every inference.
4. **Regulatory-Ready Edge Architecture:** We demonstrate the deployment of this end-to-end pipeline—quantized to INT8 for ultra-low-power ESP32-S3 hardware—integrated with a progressive alarm escalation state machine. The resulting framework natively generates FDA-compliant Model Cards and achieves 100% hazard validation under ISO 14971 standards.
